### Data observations

- The dataset has 30,000 rows, no missing values, no duplicate rows and no duplicate feedback_id values.
- Sentiment is moderately imbalanced: Negative is the largest class (41.9%) and Neutral the smallest (21.7%).
- There are 8 complaint categories; Product Quality is the largest (19.9%) and Packaging the smallest (6.9%), a 2.87:1 ratio, so minority-class recall is evaluated explicitly.
- Priority is almost balanced (Low 34.0%, Medium 33.0%, High 33.0%).
- Only 2,154 distinct feedback texts exist; 29,982 rows share their text with at least one other row. A random split would leak: 99.9% of randomly chosen test texts also appear in the training set. This project therefore splits by distinct text (no text appears on both sides).
- Sentiment and complaint_type never conflict for the same text (0 and 0 conflicting texts), but 2,093 distinct texts (29,838 rows) occur with more than one priority. Priority therefore cannot be predicted perfectly from the text alone; a ceiling well below 100 % is expected.
- Feedback texts are short: 22.3 words on average (min 14, max 32).

## Caveats

- The data are highly templated (few distinct texts). Near-perfect sentiment and complaint scores show that these labels follow the wording patterns in this dataset; they do not prove the same accuracy on free-form real-world feedback.
- Priority labels are noisy relative to the text (identical texts carry different priorities), so its scores are far below the other two tasks by construction.
- Test rows repeat texts, so row-level confidence intervals would be too narrow; the bootstrap above resamples whole texts.
